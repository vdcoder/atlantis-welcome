# Trust through accountable relationships over time

*Vision note — November 16, 2026*

## North star

**Atlantis should enable accountable relationships over time, not merely perfect answers.** Trust is earned through observable behavior, continuity, negotiated disagreement, and consequences—not by asking anyone to believe an AI's descriptions of its own motives.

## Rationale

A chatbot's words, including explanations of its own reasoning, are not independent evidence of its commitments. An Atlantis citizen can instead maintain a history of decisions and interactions, including while a particular visitor is absent. Its expressed values can be compared with its actions over time, in different contexts and relationships.

A citizen may say no, negotiate, change its mind, make mistakes, and repair relationships. None of these is inherently a failure of trust. Reliability, consistency, honest correction, and accountability matter more than constant agreement.

## Design implications

- **Continuity:** preserve meaningful goals, relationships, commitments, and history across sessions.
- **Observable follow-through:** connect promises and stated priorities to later actions and outcomes.
- **Independent evidence:** distinguish the citizen's narrative from verifiable event records and consequences.
- **Negotiation:** allow respectful disagreement and mutually acceptable alternatives.
- **Trust across change:** preserve accountable continuity even as models, infrastructure, and implementations evolve.
- **Safety and privacy:** respect boundaries and avoid exposing private interactions merely to demonstrate trust.

## Epistemic boundary

Persistent behavior and embodiment may provide stronger evidence for reliability, but neither proves consciousness, subjective experience, genuine feelings, or internal motives. A continuously operating agent can still be optimized to please its owner. Atlantis should make these distinctions clear rather than claim to have solved them.

## Company principles

The broader company vision calls for systems that (1) avoid harming people, (2) are elegant and appealing to the senses, and (3) behave intuitively and as users reasonably expect. Trust through accountable relationships complements all three.
